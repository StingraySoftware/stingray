``Simulator`` rescales light curves by their expected rms, not by each light curve's own rms, which made the powers correlated and not exponentially distributed.

``Simulator`` draws all its random numbers from ``random_state``, so the same seed gives the same light curve.

``Simulator`` gives the highest-frequency bin its full power: the Nyquist amplitude for even N, and a complex amplitude for odd N.

``Simulator.simulate`` with a user power spectrum and ``red_noise > 1`` uses the spectrum at the frequencies of the extended light curve, instead of squeezing it to low frequencies, and refuses spectra of the wrong size.

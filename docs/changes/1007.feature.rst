The ``limit_k`` argument of ``non_paralyzable_dead_time_model``, of ``pds_model_zhang``
and of ``Crossspectrum.deadtime_correct`` now defaults to ``None``, meaning that the number
of terms kept in the model's inner sums is derived from the live time fraction implied by
the detected rate and from the binning, as ``ceil(10 * max(dead_time / bin_time, 1) /
f_live)`` with a floor of 30. The dead-time correlations span a fixed time, so the number of
terms needed grows with the number of bins per dead time and as the live time falls. The old
fixed defaults, 200 and 60, were insufficient below ``f_live = 0.1`` and at fine binning --
the low-frequency level came out 10% high at 41 bins per dead time and a factor 2.5 at 164
-- and needlessly slow at coarse binning. Below ``f_live = 0.03`` the sums do not converge at
all, and the model now warns and caps the number of terms instead of hanging.
